## 31. [Bent-tailed radio galaxies as tracers of the intrafilament medium](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22378) <a id="link31"></a>

**ArXiv ID:** 2611.22378

**Authors:** Katlego Morakile, Kshitij Thorat, Etienne Bonnassieux, Tiziana Venturi, Konstantinos Kolokythas

**Abstract:** Bent-tailed radio galaxies have traditionally been associated with, and used as tracers of, the dense intracluster medium. In recent years, however, such sources have also been identified outside of clusters, with only a few discovered in low-density environments of cosmic filaments. Their occurrence in low-density environments remains rare and is still poorly understood. We announce the discovery of bent-tailed radio galaxies in galaxy cluster filaments. We have identified two bent-tailed radio galaxies, J0020-25 and J0019-26, within a filament at the redshift of $z \sim 0.1273$, and another bent-tailed radio galaxy, J0019-25, in a separate filament at $z \sim 0.1126$. We used MeerKAT observations of these bent-tailed radio galaxies to place constraints on their intrafilament medium (IFM). The effects of the filaments could be seen in the radio morphologies of these galaxies. Using faint regions in each galaxy, we estimate the intrafilament pressure, density, magnetic field strength, and total energy density. We found an IFM density of $\rho_\text{IFM} \approx 3.8\times 10^{-29}$ g cm$^{-3}$ for the surrounding medium of J0020-25 and J0019-26, and $\rho_\text{IFM} \approx (0.2 - 1.1) \times 10^{-26}$ g cm$^{-3}$ around J0019-25. We found that our estimates for the intrafilament environment around J0020-25 and J0019-26 lie within the $\rho_\text{IFM}$ literature estimates, while the density estimate found using J0019-25 is higher than the literature density estimates for the IFM and in agreement with the density of group environments. J0019-25 likely resides in a galaxy group embedded within a filament. These discoveries show the capabilities of using bent-tailed radio galaxies as probes to locate the otherwise invisible large-scale filaments.

## 32. [Cross-correlating the SPHEREx extragalactic background light with CMB lensing II: theoretical predictions confronted with lightcone simulations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21780) <a id="link32"></a>

**ArXiv ID:** 2611.21780

**Authors:** Azadeh Moradinezhad Dizgah, Zucheng Gao

**Abstract:** Cross-correlating intensity maps with the lensing of the cosmic microwave background (CMB) rejects the foregrounds and noise that do not trace the dark matter. In the near infrared, the remaining signal combines the integrated galaxy light (IGL), the intrahalo light (IHL) and several emission lines. Accurate modeling of all three is essential to forecast detectability and interpret measurements. We present simulated SPHEREx intensity maps of all three on a lightcone, painted on halos from the Hidden Valley $N$-body simulation, and the CMB-lensing convergence of the same lightcone, over $100\,{\rm deg}^2$ to $z \approx 6$. We assign each halo its lines and continuum by stellar-population synthesis, in two models whose metallicities are fixed or follow its stellar mass. Halo-model predictions from the simulation's luminosity-halo mass relations and measured clustering, without free parameters, match the auto-spectra of the IGL and lines, and their cross-spectra with lensing, across the band: at $2.2\,\mu$m, to within $5\%$ above $\ell \approx 400$ and $7\%$ above $\ell = 200$, respectively. The agreement requires nonlinear clustering: linear theory describes the cross-spectrum of a line up to $\ell \approx 1000$-$1600$ at $2.2\,\mu$m, but fails for the IGL and the convergence at multipoles of a few hundred. With the noise of a SPHEREx deep field and of Simons Observatory lensing, the unfiltered cross-spectra, each component taken alone, reach signal-to-noise ratios of $9$-$21$ for the lines, $20$ for the IGL and $15$ for the IHL. A filter that removes radial modes longer than about $600$ Mpc keeps $62\%$ of the IGL's signal-to-noise and at most $20\%$ of the lines', with fitted amplitudes within about $1\sigma$ of the predictions before and after filtering. The emission model changes a channel's intensity by up to a factor of three, but the line detectability only by tens of percent.

Using SDO/AIA and SDO/HMI observations of all four near-side passages of a long-lived active region (NOAA AR 12192), we fit a single relation that gives mean per-pixel unsigned magnetic flux from mean per-pixel 304 {\AA} intensity. The fit uses only disk-centre, flare-free intervals, and flux is the dependent variable, so the relation can be applied directly to intensity measurements. The magnetogram flux is deprojected to the radial direction before fitting, and we report the line-of-sight version for comparison. We first test the relation by fitting it on the first two passages and predicting the two later passages, which were not used in that fit; the relation adopted for the reconstruction is then fitted to all four passages. We then apply the same relation to STEREO-A/EUVI 304 {\AA} observations, scaling the EUVI intensities to AIA-equivalent boundary intensities inferred from the adjacent near-side flux measurements, and so reconstruct the unsigned flux history while the region was out of view from Earth. The test on the later passages gives a median log-space residual of +0.052 dex, a root-mean-square error of 0.083 dex, and a median absolute fractional error of 14% over 272 samples that were not used in the fit. Because a single cross-instrument scale factor cannot match both ends of a far-side interval, the absolute level of each reconstructed segment carries an uncertainty of several tens of per cent; it is the shape of the far-side curve, rather than its level, that the EUVI observations constrain.

## 101. [A Catalog of Young Stellar Objects in the Lagoon Nebula](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21962) <a id="link101"></a>

**ArXiv ID:** 2611.21962

**Authors:** L. M. Rebull, L. Venuti, A. M. Cody

**Abstract:** M8, the Lagoon Nebula, has been known for more than 100 years as a region forming stars. It is (in projection) very close to if not in the Galactic Bulge. As a result, the source surface density is very high and source confusion is a substantial challenge. Because of the location, there have been many surveys that serendipitously observed M8, in addition to studies specifically targeting it. But to this point, there has been no effort to resolve source confusion across all the existing catalogs, spanning many different wavelengths, sensitivities, and spatial resolutions. We have done all of this source reconciliation, and present the merged catalog here. The net result is 3019 YSOs with largely photometric data and/or YSO selection from ~50 different origins, primarily ranging from $g$-band through 25 um, but with a significant number of X-ray sources, and some data of limited sensitivity out through 70-450 um. The catalog includes several flags that enable users to select, e.g., the YSOs that have been identified as young by the most studies, or the YSOs with the most multi-wavelength counterparts, or the ones with the clearest IR excesses. We use this catalog in a companion paper; it will also be of use in, e.g., future SPHEREx and Roman work.
